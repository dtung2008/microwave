1.1 Where the lines live

Three claims for today

  1. A "50 Ω trace" is a geometry problem.
    Width over height over permittivity. Today you compute the width: 1.11 mm on our board.
  2. The wavelength obeys a permittivity that does not exist.
    ε_eff — a weighted average of board and air. Use ε_r instead and every stub lands 13% short. Hour 3 does it on purpose.
  3. A hollow pipe out-guides every cable — above its cutoff.
    f_c = c/2a. Below: the wave is refused. Just above: dispersion turns on you. We price both.
1.2 The microstrip

The cross-section — fields in two worlds at once

Printed, not machined. Solderable — which is why the whole front-end lives on it.

The wave straddles two media → it settles on a compromise speed v = c/√εeff. Our board: h = 0.508 mm, t = 35 μm copper, tan δ = 0.0037 (RO4350B).

1.2 The compromise

εeff — a filling factor, not a material

εeff ≡ C/Cair   ·   (εr+1)/2  ≤  εeff  ≤  εr
  • Same geometry's capacitance with and without the dielectric — "how much the board actually helped."
  • Very wide strip: field buried in the board → εeff → εr. Vanishing strip: field split evenly with the air → (εr+1)/2.
  • Our 50 Ω line: εeff = 2.74 between floor 2.24 and ceiling 3.48.

"Quasi-TEM" is a legal lie. True TEM (transverse electromagnetic — E, H both ⊥ travel) can't straddle two media (it would need two speeds at once); the real mode grows tiny longitudinal fields. On a thin board (h ≪ λ: 0.508 mm is λ/33 at 20 GHz) they are second-order small — the lie holds, then fails gracefully: dispersion (slide 9).

1.3 Hammerstad

Analysis: geometry → electricals  (u = w/h)

εeff = (εr+1)/2 + (εr−1)/2 · (1 + 12/u)−1/2

u ≤ 1:   Z₀ = (60/√εeff) · ln(8/u + u/4)

u ≥ 1:   Z₀ = 120π / [√εeff · (u + 1.393 + 0.667·ln(u + 1.444))]

  • Not arbitrary: a curve fit to Wheeler's exact conformal map (1965). Narrow limit = a wire over a plane (the ln); wide limit = parallel plate + fringing.
  • Real copper is 35 μm thick — Wheeler's correction: weff = w + (t/π)(1 + ln(2h/t)) = +49 μm here. Moves Z₀ ~1 Ω; the homework's 2% referee notices.

1.4 Dispersion

When the quasi-TEM lie catches up

As f rises the field retreats from the air fringe into the substrate → εeff climbs toward εr:

fε_eff (skrf, Kirschning–Jansen)
1 GHz2.709
10 GHz2.747
20 GHz2.810

Quasi-static hand value 2.736: within 1% to ≈13 GHz on this stackup.

Thin boards buy you quasi-TEM. h = 0.508 mm keeps the mode honest through X-band — that is why the course board is a 20-mil core.

Cut lengths for εeff at your operating frequency, not at DC. Lecture 9's war story: a fabricated filter 4% low — every length cut for the DC value.

1.4 The cousins

CPW and stripline — one virtue each

CPW (coplanar waveguide)

  • Ground on the top surface — always one bond-wire away (MMICs love it)
  • Z₀ set by gap/width ratio — an extra degree of freedom
  • Price: more conductor edge → more conductor loss

Stripline

  • Homogeneous sandwich → true TEM: εeff = εr, no microstrip-style dispersion, fully shielded
  • Price: you can't solder to a buried layer — routing, not components

Decision in one line: components on microstrip · dense shielded routing on stripline · MMIC interfaces on CPW.

Hour 2

The Hollow Pipe

Why no-conductor guides · cutoff and the mode zoo · the ω-β diagram and the price of dispersion · the loss shootout

2.1 Why hollow pipes guide

Fit half-sines between the walls

  • Metal wall demands Etan = 0 at both walls, spacing a → fit half sine waves: transverse wavenumber kc = π/a.
  • The frequency must pay for transverse and forward wavenumber:
β² = k² − kc²  ⇒  β = (2π/c)·√(f² − fc²),   fc = c/2a

k = 2πf/c total wavenumber; β = forward propagation constant (lecture 2's γ, lossless).

f < fc: β goes imaginary — e−jβz → e−κz. The wave is refused, not absorbed: no loss anywhere, energy stored and returned. Hour 3 watches a lossless simulation kill a wave in 1 cm.

2.1 First principles

Separation of variables gives the whole zoo

fc(m,n) = (c/2)·√( (m/a)² + (n/b)² )
  • TEmn (transverse electric — E strictly ⊥ travel, some H along the guide); TMmn (transverse magnetic — the reverse).
  • m, n = number of half-sine variations across a, b. The fast version was the m=1, n=0 row.
  • Solve: sin(mπx/a)·sin(nπy/b) patterns from Etan = 0 on all four walls — lecture 2's boundary conditions, now in 2-D.

TE₁₀ is the dominant mode — lowest fc. For a standard a ≈ 2b guide, the next two (TE₂₀ at c/a, TE₀₁ at c/2b) arrive together at exactly 2·fc → one octave of single-mode operation.

2.3 Dispersion

The ω-β diagram — the slope is the speed of energy

  • Slope from origin = vp = ω/β > c — no alarm, phase carries no information.
  • Tangent slope = vg = c·√(1−(fc/f)²) < c — the energy speed.
  • vp·vg = c². Near cutoff: energy crawls, phase sprints.

2.4 Loss

Three ways to lose power — three fingerprints

  • Conductor: current crowds into skin depth δ = 1/√(πfμσ); Rs = √(πfμρ) grows as √f. Microstrip estimate: αc ≈ Rs/(Z₀w) — a 1 mm strip pays dearly; centimeters of waveguide wall pay little.
  • Dielectric: tan δ of stored energy per radian → αd grows as f (more radians per meter). The waveguide's dielectric is air — line item deleted.
  • Radiation / surface waves: the open structure's tax at discontinuities; the closed pipe's is zero by topology.

Np vs dB, once and forever: fields decay e−αℓ, power e−2αℓ → dB = 8.686·αℓ (= 20·log₁₀e, not 10·log₁₀e). The homework's module 3 walks this gate consciously.

Waveguide beats the good board 84×; the cheap board loses 91% of the power (10.47 dB) in 30 cm. Add power handling — air breakdown in WR-90 is tens of kW vs a microstrip edge arcing at tens of W — and you know why radar transmitters bolt brass pipes to their dishes.

2.4 War story

The patch array that fed the substrate

A 10 GHz patch-array prototype: elegant corporate feed, gain 4 dB below prediction. Weeks spent blaming the patches. The culprit: ~40 cm of cumulative FR-4 feed line quietly eating ~40% of the transmit power before any of it reached an antenna. At their last project's 2.4 GHz the same feed had been fine — αd scales with f, and intuition built at WiFi frequencies does not transfer to X-band. The fix was a Rogers respin and a lesson invoiced at one board spin.

The frequency ladder: 2.4 GHz — FR-4 is fine, nobody buys waveguide · 10 GHz — Rogers for the front-end, pipe for the long runs and the kilowatts · 77 GHz — lines are millimeters, the antenna moves onto the package (lecture 13).
