Transmission-Line Theory
The Wire Becomes a Component

Principles (100 min) → break → Tools: the line engine in NumPy, scikit-rf as referee → homework: the feed line that lies

Reading: Steer, Microwave and RF Design Vol. 2, ch. 2–3 (free) · Pozar ch. 2 · Orfanidis ch. 10–11 (free)

1.1 The promise lecture 1 made

Three claims for today

  1. A line is a ladder of infinitesimal circuits — and two numbers summarize it.
    From R′, L′, G′, C′ (per-meter) fall Z₀ and γ = α + jβ. Everything else is consequences.
  2. The line enforces a ratio; loads that refuse it reflect.
    Z₀ is the V/I a traveling wave must carry. Γ measures the disagreement.
  3. A length of line transforms impedance — and that is a feature.
    λ/4 inverts. Today: a feed line lying by 19 dB, and a match good to |Γ| ≈ 10⁻¹⁶.

The test article all day: 10 m of coax to a 2.4 GHz antenna — λ = 83 mm, so the feed is 120 wavelengths long.

1.2 The RLGC cell

One slice dz of any two-conductor line

Past ~λ/10, V and I differ from point to point — so write Kirchhoff on a slice dz and let dz → 0. The ladder is not a cartoon of the field theory; for TEM (transverse electromagnetic) lines it is the field theory in circuit clothes.

1.2 The telegrapher's equations

KVL + KCL on the slice, then the limit

dV/dz = −(R′ + jωL′)·I     dI/dz = −(G′ + jωC′)·V

(KVL/KCL = Kirchhoff's voltage/current laws — used once more, on their way out)

Differentiate one, substitute the other — the wave equation, and the two numbers of the day:

d²V/dz² = γ²V    γ = √((R′+jωL′)(G′+jωC′)) = α + jβ     Z₀ = √((R′+jωL′)/(G′+jωC′))

Solutions e∓γz: two waves, one each way; α (nepers/m) shrinks them, β (rad/m) turns their phase; λ = 2π/β. Every uniform line, fully described by γ and Z₀.

Named for the 1850s engineers who watched undersea cables smear Morse code — the first signal-integrity crisis.

1.3 The two numbers, with numbers

The course cable — an RG-58-class coax at 2.4 GHz

per-meter cellvalue
R′10 Ω/m
L′250 nH/m
G′0.45 mS/m
C′100 pF/m

R′, G′ frozen at their 2.4 GHz values (skin effect makes R′ ∝ √f — lecture 5's fine print). A modeling choice, stated out loud.

Z₀ = √(L′/C′) = √2500 = 50 Ω — that's where 50 lives
vp = 1/√(L′C′) = 2×10⁸ m/s = 0.667c
λ = vp/f = 83.33 mm → 10 m feed = 120 λ
α ≈ R′/2Z₀ + G′Z₀/2 = 0.111 Np/m = 0.966 dB/m

With loss, hour 3 prints Z₀ = 50.0001 − j0.0589 Ω — the imaginary part is loss's fingerprint. 1 Np = 8.686 dB.

1.3 What Z₀ actually is

An enforced ratio — not a resistor

Z₀ is not measurable with an ohmmeter. At DC the meter reads R′ℓ plus the far-end termination; 50 Ω appears nowhere. Z₀ dissipates nothing — it is the V/I exchange rate a traveling wave must carry.

Terminate the line in Z₀ and the arriving wave finds its ratio already satisfied — nothing reflects. That single sentence is the whole theory of matching.

  • Why 50 Ω? Air-line power handling peaks near 30 Ω, loss bottoms near 77 Ω — 50 is the committee compromise. (75 Ω survives in broadcast, where loss wins.)
  • 0.667c — the electrons? No: they drift at mm/s. The energy rides the fields between the conductors; 0.667c = 1/√εr, polyethylene's εr ≈ 2.25.
1.4 Reflection

Two ratios, one pair of terminals — Γ resolves the standoff

The wave carries V/I = Z₀; the load demands V/I = ZL. A backward wave appears so the totals obey the load (its current flows backward — it subtracts):

Γ = V⁻/V⁺ = (ZL − Z₀)/(ZL + Z₀)

Γ = −1

short: all back, flipped (V pinned to 0)

Γ = +1

open: all back, upright (I pinned to 0)

Γ = 0

matched: ratio satisfied, silence

|Γ| = 1

pure jX: stores, never absorbs — a mirror with a phase

The course antenna, met today and matched in lecture 3: ZL = 36 − j21 Ω → Γ = 0.285∠−110.0° — 8.1% of the power comes back.

1.4 The four dialects

One number, four suits — make the translations reflexes

0.285

|Γ| — reflected amplitude ratio

1.80

SWR (standing wave ratio) = (1+|Γ|)/(1−|Γ|)

10.90 dB

return loss = −20 log₁₀|Γ| (big is good)

0.37 dB

mismatch loss = −10 log₁₀(1−|Γ|²) (small is good)

Power split: |Γ|² = 8.1% reflected, 1 − |Γ|² = 91.9% delivered. A datasheet's "S11 = −10.9 dB" and "RL = 10.9 dB" are the same fact — signs are conventions, not physics.

Two gammas now live on your page. γ = α + jβ is the propagation constant; Γ is the reflection coefficient. The homework code refuses the pun by name: gamma_per_m vs refl.

1.4 The reflected-power picture

Where do the 8.1% go?

  • Back down the line, toward the generator — reflections are not destroyed, they are re-routed.
  • Matched generator (a 50 Ω source — this course's standing assumption): the source resistance absorbs them. Heat, quietly.
  • Mismatched generator: the wave reflects again — and again. That multiple-bounce story is hour 3's bounce diagram; run to steady state, it is the standing-wave formulas. One physics, two views.

Why "SWR" survives as a word: the first RF instrument was a slotted line — it could only measure the envelope. And it still matters: |V|max = (1+|Γ|)·|V⁺| arcs connectors; (1+|Γ|)× current heats finals. SWR is the stress dialect of Γ.

1.5 Recap

Hour 1 in three sentences

  • A line is the dz → 0 limit of an RLGC ladder; the telegrapher's equations compress it to γ and Z₀ — ours: 50 Ω, 0.667c, 0.97 dB/m.
  • Z₀ is an enforced ratio, not a resistor — and a load that disagrees reflects Γ = (ZL−Z₀)/(ZL+Z₀). Our antenna: 8.1% back.
  • |Γ| ⇔ SWR ⇔ return loss ⇔ mismatch loss: one number, four dialects.

The envelope that stands still · the tangent transformation · loss launders reflections · a step in the time domain

2.1 Standing-wave anatomy

The wave moves; the envelope does not

V(ℓ) = V⁺ejβℓ(1 + Γe−j2βℓ), ℓ measured from the load. Measured max/min = 1.2851/0.7149 = 1.7976; SWR from |Γ| said 1.7976 — two routes, one number. The pattern repeats every λ/2, not λ.

2.1 The crank diagram — next week's Smith chart, in embryo

1 + Γe−j2βℓ: a crank sweeping clockwise

  • Arm along +1: voltage maximum. Arm against 1: minimum.
  • Full revolution = 2βℓ advancing 2π = ℓ advancing λ/2 — the factor of 2 everyone trips on once.
  • Crank crosses the real axis twice per lap → Zin is purely real every λ/4, alternating Z₀/SWR and Z₀·SWR.

Everything a lossless line does to an impedance is a rotation of Γ. Lecture 3 gives this picture its famous coordinate grid.

2.2 Input impedance

The tangent transformation

Zin = Z₀ (ZL + jZ₀ tan βℓ) / (Z₀ + jZL tan βℓ)
  • tan has period π → Zin repeats every λ/2 (the crank's period, as it must be).
  • tan sweeps 0 → ∞ each period → within λ/2 of any load, Zin visits capacitive, inductive, small-R, large-R — a cable is an impedance transformer whether you ordered one or not.
  • Lossy version: replace j·tan βℓ with tanh γℓ — the form your homework engine must use (np.tan explodes at the λ/4 pole; np.tanh shrugs).

Change a jumper's length by 3 cm at 2.4 GHz and you have redesigned the input network. Nothing "did nothing" — it was measured at the one frequency where the tangent hides.

2.2 The special cases — each one a tool

λ/2 repeats · λ/4 inverts · stubs synthesize reactance

ℓ = λ/2

Zin = ZL

the line repeats the load — invisible (at that one frequency)

ℓ = λ/4

Zin = Z₀²/ZL

the inverter: large↔small, capacitive↔inductive, real→real

stubs

jZ₀tan βℓ / −jZ₀cot βℓ

short / open spur = any reactance you want (lecture 3's parts bin)

The quarter-wave transformer: to match a real R to Z₀, build the λ/4 from line of ZT = √(Z₀R) — then Zin = ZT²/R = Z₀ exactly. Two lines of algebra, one of the most-built structures in microwave engineering.

2.2 The fix, for our complex antenna

Walk to a real plane, then invert — exact by construction

  1. λ/4 matches real loads only — and 36 − j21 Ω is not.
  2. Zin is purely real where the crank crosses the axis: at a voltage minimum, Z = Z₀/SWR = 27.8 Ω; at a maximum, Z₀·SWR = 89.9 Ω.
  3. Spacer of plain 50 Ω line to the first minimum: 0.0973 λ = 8.11 mm.
  4. Quarter wave (20.83 mm) of ZT = √(50 × 27.8) = 37.29 Ω.

Checker prints |Γ(f₀)| ≈ 7×10⁻¹⁷ — machine epsilon: the algebra is exact, the referee confirms arithmetic. Homework hint: design with the ideal line's β — a 10⁻¹⁰ criterion notices sixth digits.

"Narrowband" is a number, not a slur: measured 10-dB-RL bandwidth 2.33 GHz (97% of f₀); 20-dB, 577 MHz (24%). Lecture 6 makes it wider.

2.3 Loss

Where dB/m comes from — and what 10 m costs

α ≈ R′/2Z₀ + G′Z₀/2 = 0.100 + 0.011 = 0.111 Np/m  →  × 8.686 = 0.966 dB/m
  • Amplitude falls e−αℓ, power e−2αℓ; our 10 m feed: 9.66 dB one way.
  • Conductor loss ÷ Z₀, dielectric loss × Z₀ — the two mechanisms pull Z₀'s optimum in opposite directions (the 30-vs-77 story behind "50 Ω").

The power ledger at f₀, of 1 W incident (homework module 2 makes it exact): delivered 99.3 mW · reflected back out 0.95 mW · cable heat 899.7 mW. Delivered sits 10.03 dB below incident = 9.66 (line) + 0.37 (mismatch) — the taxes add in dB.

The invariant that referees module 2: loss off → |Γ|² + delivered = 1 to 10⁻¹⁶. Energy conservation certifies your code — no library required.

2.3 The feed line that lies

Every dB of cable flatters the load by two

RLin ≈ RLantenna + 2 × line loss
= 10.90 + 19.33 ≈ 30.2 dB

measured: 30.09 dB — the 0.1 dB gap is the cable's slightly complex Z₀

The antenna is mediocre (SWR 1.8); the instrument says excellent (30 dB). The reflection paid the attenuation toll twice. A long-enough lossy cable makes any load — even an open — look matched. Measure at the plane you care about, or correct for the cable. (VNA = vector network analyzer — lecture 4 tours one.)

2.4 Time domain

The bounce diagram — a step, bouncing geometrically

  • Launch = divider against Z₀, not the load — causality: 50/(25+50) = 0.667 V.
  • Each round trip × ΓLΓs = −1/6 — geometric convergence to the DC divider 0.857 V.
  • First edge overshoots by +16.7%.

On a scope this is called ringing; its true name is |Γ|, its period the round trip 2ℓ/vp. "Electrically long" in time-domain dress: rise time < round trip — the digital twin of λ/10. Run it to t → ∞ and the phasor formulas reappear.

2.4 War story

The half-wavelength jumper that "did nothing"

  • Dual-band site: low band at f₀, second band at 1.5f₀. A damaged 50 Ω jumper is replaced by a 75 Ω video cable of the same length — exactly λ/2 at f₀.
  • Bench check at f₀: nothing changes. Of course — a half-wave line repeats its load regardless of its own Z₀. The check certified nothing.
  • At 1.5f₀ the same jumper is 3λ/4 — an odd quarter wave: a full inverter through 75 Ω. The 50 Ω system reads 75²/50 = 112.5 Ω — SWR 2.25, return loss 8.3 dB, margin gone.

The tangent is periodic: a line's behavior at one frequency certifies that frequency only. Nobody mismeasured — the measurement was taken exactly where the tangent hides. Hour 3 commits this sin on purpose, with a transformer.

2.5 Recap

Hour 2 in three sentences

  • Standing waves: envelope max/min is SWR; the crank rotates 2βℓ, everything repeats every λ/2, and the real-axis crossings (Z₀/SWR, Z₀·SWR) host the λ/4 fix — exact by construction.
  • Loss launders reflections: RLin ≈ RLL + 2 × line loss — our cable lies by 19 dB, and 900 mW of every watt becomes heat.
  • A step bounces geometrically; ringing is |Γ| speaking the time domain.
