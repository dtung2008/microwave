1.1 Why this lecture exists

Three claims for today

  1. One tangent is the whole theory.
    Ω = tan(βl) maps lumped synthesis onto lines exactly — today: 3×10⁻¹⁴ dB from the closed form.
  2. Kuroda is an identity, not an approximation.
    The unbuildable becomes buildable; equivalence measured at 7×10⁻¹⁶, every frequency.
  3. Distributed filters are periodic.
    tan repeats ⇒ your filter repeats. You will predict the reentrant passband before sweeping.
1.2 Richards' transformation

Stubs are lumped elements on a warped axis

Stub input impedances (lecture 2):

1.2 Richards' transformation

Why the whole response maps — and what it costs

Commensurate-network theorem (Richards 1948): if every line has the SAME θ, every S-parameter is a rational function of t = j·tan θ — a lumped network's mathematics, in Ω.

Sketch: each line's ABCD = cos θ·[[1, jZt̂],[jt̂/Z, 1]] with t̂ = tan θ — polynomial in tan θ; cascades preserve rationality. ("Commensurate" = all lines equal electrical length.)

1.3 Kuroda's identities — the problem

Richards hands layout a network it must refuse

  • Series stubs do not exist in microstrip. A series stub must interrupt the signal trace, run sideways, and short to ground — over an unbroken ground plane. There is nothing to draw.
  • And Richards piles all N elements onto one node — stubs sprouting from a point couple to each other: an un-modeled blob.

War story: the series stub "approximated" as a meandered gap — a gap is a series capacitor, wrong element entirely. The board that came back was a coupler nobody ordered.

shunt open stubs: easy. series short stubs: not in this technology.

1.3 Kuroda's identities — the fix

A stub can tunnel through a unit element — derived in six lines

Unit element (UE): a line of the same θ, impedance of our choosing (t = tan θ):

UE(Z₁)·SeriesStub(Z₂) = cos θ · 1jt(Z₁+Z₂)jt/Z₁1 − t²Z₂/Z₁
ShuntStub(Ẑ₂)·UE(Ẑ₁) = cos θ · 1jtẐ₁jt(1/Ẑ₁+1/Ẑ₂)1 − t²Ẑ₁/Ẑ₂

Match entries: Ẑ₁ = Z₁+Z₂ = n²Z₁,   Ẑ₂ = Z₁(Z₁+Z₂)/Z₂ = n²Z₁²/Z₂, with n² = 1 + Z₂/Z₁. Exact at every θ — every frequency.

Sanity limit θ→0: small series L trades for small shunt C, differing only at O(θ²) — believable; the matrices make it exact.

1.3 Kuroda's identities

The family: four identities, two workhorses

  • #1, #2 (transformer-free): series short-circuited stub ⇄ shunt open-circuited stub, traded across a unit element; both impedances scale by n² = 1 + Z₂/Z₁. (You derived one; the other is the same algebra run backward.)
  • #3, #4: swap open ⇄ short stub species — at the price of an ideal n²:1 transformer (Pozar Table 8.7). Stated for completeness; ideal transformers are one more component you cannot buy.

Kuroda is an identity, not an approximation. Hour 3 measures the swapped network against the original: max ||S21| difference| = 7×10⁻¹⁶ — float64 round-off, at every frequency, DC to daylight. (Contrast hour 2's J-inverter step, which IS narrowband.)

2.1 Stepped-impedance lowpass

Fat line, thin line — a ladder in sausage form

Short high-Z line ≈ series L; short low-Z line ≈ shunt C:

βli = giR₀/Zh (L)  ·  βli = giZl/R₀ (C)

Butterworth N=3 at 3 GHz, Z_h = 120 Ω, Z_l = 20 Ω → 23.9° / 45.8° / 23.9°. That middle 45.8° is not "short" — the approximation is already straining.

2.2 Coupled-line bandpass

The coupled section — lecture 7's investment pays out

Feed diagonally, leave the other ends open. Even/odd decomposition gives the 2-port:

Z₁₁ = −j·(Z0e+Z0o)/2 · cot θ
Z₁₂ = −j·(Z0e−Z0o)/2 · 1/sin θ

At θ = 90°: Z₁₁ = 0 — a pure impedance inverter, K = (Z0e − Z0o)/2. The coupling strength IS the impedance split.

2.2 Coupled-line bandpass

The design procedure: g → J → (Z0e, Z0o)

Lecture 8 said: a bandpass = resonators coupled by inverters, strengths from the g-values. For fractional bandwidth Δ:

Z₀J₁ = √(πΔ/2g₀g₁)  ·  Z₀Jk = (πΔ/2)/√(gk−1gk)  ·  Z₀JN+1 = √(πΔ/2gNgN+1)
Z0e = Z₀(1 + JZ₀ + (JZ₀)²)    Z0o = Z₀(1 − JZ₀ + (JZ₀)²)

Why do the ends carry square roots? End inverters couple a resonator to a termination (g₀ = 1), not to another resonator — hw6's external-Q asymmetry, wearing a new hat.

2.2 Honesty clause #1

The J-inverter picture is narrowband — and it bills you

  • The g-values: exact. The Z0e/Z0o algebra: exact given J.
  • But "λ/4 resonator + ideal inverter" is exact only at f₀ — first order in (f−f₀)/f₀.
  • hw8's lumped transformation was an exact substitution: edges hit to machine precision. This one is a Taylor expansion.

Measured (hour 3): 0.5-dB bandwidth 9.83% vs 10% designed; ripple leaks to 0.57 dB at the 2.28 GHz edge.

Narrower Δ → nearly exact; 20% → visibly off. Honest fixes: pad Δ and re-measure, or exact corrections (Matthaei–Young–Jones) — never hand-tweaking Z0e's until the plot looks nice.

2.3 Honesty clause #2 — reentrance

The Richards circle: θ goes around, f goes along

cot, csc, tan: all periodic in θ with period 180°; θ is linear in f. Therefore:

Every commensurate filter's response is periodic in frequency.
Stub lowpass (λ/8 at f_c): passband again over 3f_c–5f_c (9–15 GHz).
Coupled-line bandpass (λ/4 at f₀): zero at 2f₀, passband again at 3f₀ = 7.2 GHz — |S21| = 0 dB — then 5f₀, forever.

2.3 Reentrance — design consequences

Three sentences to write down

  • Your stopband spec is only enforceable between the passband and the first reentrance — "40 dB ultimate rejection" is not a thing a commensurate filter can promise.
  • A strong interferer near 3f₀ disqualifies this topology at the architecture level.
  • The system fix is honest cascading: a lowpass mops up the bandpass's second life — and you check the lowpass's own reentrance too.

Lumped filters never did this — ω runs on a line, not around a circle. Reentrance is the one genuinely new failure mode you bought with the copper. Hour 3's deliberate bug lives here; so does homework Q1.

2.4 The ideal-vs-EM gap

What the field solver knows and the ideal sweep cannot

The 2f₀ zero needs both modes at half-wave together. They aren't: the zero splits, energy leaks between the split zeros → the spurious passband near 2f₀.

  • Mode-velocity split → 2f₀ spur (the classic coupled-microstrip defect)
  • Dispersion (L5): lengths cut at quasi-static ε_eff are long at f₀ → response slides down
  • Discontinuities: open-end fringing, junction storage, step scattering

War story: the fabricated filter 4% low — every length cut with ε_eff at DC, not f₀. The ideal sweep couldn't catch it; the ideal sweep was the bug.
