1.1 Why this lecture exists

Three claims for today

  1. Detection is a decision under uncertainty.
    Pd (detection probability) and Pfa (false-alarm probability) replace the 13 dB hand-wave — which turns out to have been nearly honest.
  2. The false-alarm mathematics is exact — invertible by hand.
    One Rayleigh closed form sets every threshold. One factor-of-2 slip in its exponent turns 10⁻⁶ into 10⁻³. Hour 3 commits it on purpose.
  3. A fixed threshold dies when it leaves the lab.
    CFAR (constant false-alarm rate) — estimate the noise where the target isn't — and its measurable tax and blind spots.
1.2 Radar cross section

σ is how a target scatters — the honest version

σ (RCS, radar cross section) is defined through the equation: the area that, re-radiating the intercepted power isotropically, explains the echo you receive. Units m²; in dB: dBsm (dB re 1 m²).

  • Not a silhouette: a flat plate face-on returns 4πA²/λ² — a 30 × 30 cm plate at X-band is ~110 m², bigger than the airliner's whole-body number.
  • Tilt the same plate 30° and the flash goes somewhere you aren't. σ is a phasor sum of scattering centers — inlets, junctions, tail flats.
  • Consequence: σ swings 20–30 dB with aspect and re-phases with frequency. Lecture 1's "σ ≈ 40 m²" — the ≈ was hiding a dance.
1.2 Radar cross section

The dBsm ladder — who reflects what

The drone problem, again: drone and large bird share a rung. Lecture 15's micro-Doppler separates them; today we fight to see the rung at all.

X-band weather radars routinely image insect swarms — −40 dBsm, in numbers. Everything reflects; the question is always relative to what (wait for the clutter slide).

1.2 Radar cross section

Stealth has exactly two levers (Balanis, RCS-reduction chapter [R10])

Shaping — redirect, don't absorb. Kill the corner-like retroreflectors; facet or blend so the specular flash misses the monostatic receiver. Why stealth jets look the way they do — and why bistatic radar is the counter-argument.

Absorption — RAM (radar-absorbing material) turns induced currents into heat. Effective, narrowband-ish, heavy, maintenance-hungry.

Both levers buy dB of σ — and the fourth root pays them back at 25 cents on the dollar:

−30 dB of σ → range ÷ 1030/40 ≈ 5.6×

A serious program removes 99.9% of the echo to cut detection range by ~6×. Worthwhile — the fights it changes are real — but not magic. (Lecture 1 said it; now you know what the 30 dB costs to obtain.)

1.3 Detection as hypothesis testing

Two densities, one threshold, two ways to be wrong

Slide T right: Pfa collapses, Pd sags. Left: the screen fills. The threshold cannot fix the overlap — only SNR can. That is what the whole course was buying.

1.3 The densities

After the envelope detector: Rayleigh and Rician

I and Q: independent Gaussians, variance σ² each — total noise power N = 2σ² (flag this 2; it is today's landmine). Envelope r = √(I²+Q²):

H₀: p(r) = (r/σ²)·e−r²/2σ² (Rayleigh)
H₁: Rician — Rayleigh bent around the echo amplitude A

Why envelope? The echo's phase is unknowable (range mod λ/2 = 1.5 cm sets it). Discard the phase, keep the energy — that is why Rayleigh, not Gaussian, runs today.

At 6 dB the densities overlap hopelessly — no threshold saves you. Hour 2: how many dB honesty costs.

1.4 The false alarm, exactly

One integral that closes — and inverts by hand

Pfa = ∫T∞ (r/σ²) e−r²/2σ² dr = e−T²/2σ²   ⇒   T = √( N·ln(1/Pfa) )

(N = 2σ² = the noise power your lecture-1 budget computes as kT₀BF)

Pfa = 10⁻⁶, unit noise power: T = 3.7169 — the threshold sits 11.40 dB above the mean noise power (3.72× the rms envelope). Hour 3 measures it with 10⁶ draws.

Why 10⁻⁶? Count decisions: B = 1 MHz ≈ 10⁶ independent looks/s. 10⁻⁶ = one false blip per second — already enough that real systems add m-of-n confirmation. 10⁻³ = a thousand per second: a white screen. Pfa is set by the decision rate, and the rate is set by B.

1.4 The knife edge

Pfa is an exponential of a square

Nothing else in this course responds to half a decibel like this.

The factor-of-2 landmine: put the total noise power N in the per-channel σ² slot and the exponent halves — Pfa becomes its own square root. 10⁻⁶ → 10⁻³, silently, with plausible-looking numbers everywhere.

Consequence for practice: never ship a threshold you have not measured. Hour 3 commits this exact bug and lets a million Monte Carlo draws catch it.

2.1 Detection probability

Pd is a Rician tail — Marcum's Q function

Pd = Q₁( √(2·SNR), √(2·ln(1/Pfa)) )
  • Does not close in elementary functions — scipy evaluates it exactly (noncentral χ²); the homework toolkit wraps it as marcum_pd, the referee.
  • The curve is steep: at Pfa = 10⁻⁶, Pd goes 0.25 → 0.68 → 0.97 as SNR goes 10 → 12 → 14 dB.
  • Steepness is good news — it makes "detection range" a meaningful number instead of a 10 km smear.

Design flow: the customer states (Pd, Pfa) → statistics give SNRmin → the radar equation (slide 2) turns SNRmin into Rmax. The missing middle step is one formula away.

2.1 Albersheim's approximation

The whiteboard version — with a datasheet

A = ln(0.62/Pfa)  ·  B = ln( Pd/(1−Pd) )
SNRdB = −5·log₁₀N + ( 6.2 + 4.54/√(N+0.44) ) · log₁₀( A + 0.12AB + 1.7B )

Validity envelope (state it like a spec): ~0.2 dB for 10⁻⁷ ≤ Pfa ≤ 10⁻³,  0.1 ≤ Pd ≤ 0.9,  1 ≤ N ≤ 8096. Linear detector, N non-coherent pulses, nonfluctuating target.

An approximation with a stated envelope is an instrument; one without is a rumor. The homework checker measures the envelope — including the corner at (Pd = 0.1, Pfa = 10⁻⁷) where it frays to 0.75 dB. Envelopes have corners; measure, don't worship.

2.1 The audit

Lecture 1's 13 dB, decompressed

(Pd = 0.9, Pfa = 10⁻⁶, N = 1): Albersheim 13.11 dB · exact 13.18 dB. The hand-wave was the compressed form of this spec all along.

target13 dB bar (L1)honest (0.9, 10⁻⁶)
airliner32.65 km32.44 km
fighter12.98 km12.90 km
drone4.11 km4.08 km (×0.9934)

What the hand-wave hid was not range — it was meaning: at exactly 13 dB the delivered Pd = 0.8744. "Detectable to 4.11 km" always meant "an 87–90% coin per look." And the coin degrades fast: Pd 0.9 → 0.5 spans ~1.9 dB ≈ half a kilometer of sky (R⁴: ~3.9 dB/km at 4 km).

2.2 Fluctuation

Swerling — one honest slide

  • Case 0/5: steady echo — everything so far.
  • Cases 1–2: many comparable scatterers — the echo power is exponential (scan-to-scan / pulse-to-pulse).
  • Cases 3–4: one dominant scatterer + chorus.
Swerling 1, N = 1:   Pd = Pfa 1/(1+SNR)

At (0.9, 10⁻⁶): SNR = ln Pfa/ln Pd − 1 → 21.1 dB — 8 dB more than the steady target. Demanding 90% forces you to fund the fades.

In range: ×0.63 — the honest drone range drops 4.08 → ~2.6 km if it Rayleigh-fades. (At Pd = 0.5 the gap nearly vanishes: fluctuation punishes ambition.)

Operational reflex: any quoted detection range gets three questions — what Pd, what Pfa, which Swerling?

2.3 Integration

Why 10 pulses ≠ 10× range

Coherent (before the detector; L15's FFT does this): voltages add, noise powers add → SNR × N exactly. Ten pulses = +10 dB.

Non-coherent (after the detector): phases are gone; part of the gain is spent re-detecting.

for (0.9, 10⁻⁶)SNR/pulse
1 pulse13.18 dB
10 coherent3.18 dB
10 non-coherent5.27 dB (2.1 dB tax)

Range rides the fourth root:

10 pulses coherent → ×1010/40 = 1.78× range
non-coherent → 1.58×

To double the drone's range: +12 dB after taxes — 16 coherent or ~25 non-coherent pulses. Integration is how radars afford detection; R⁴ keeps every route expensive.

2.4 Why CFAR exists

The coastal radar whose Pfa was set in the lab

Threshold verified against thermal noise at 10⁻⁶. On the coast, sea clutter raises the floor 20 dB. The exponent divides by 100:

Pfa = (10⁻⁶)1/100 = 10−0.06 = 0.87

Not 10⁻⁴. Eighty-seven percent. ~870,000 false alarms per second at B = 1 MHz. The screen goes white; the operator turns the gain down; the radar detects nothing at all.

Nobody mis-soldered anything. The threshold was a constant in a world that isn't. Note the violence of the exponential: 20 dB of floor moved Pfa five orders of magnitude — and then saturation.

2.4 CA-CFAR

Estimate the noise where the target isn't

Square-law domain (power z = r², exponential under noise — note the domain change). The training sum is Gamma; the tail integral closes: Pfa = (1 + α/N)−N ⇒ α = N·(Pfa−1/N − 1), N = total training cells.

CFAR loss = 10·log₁₀( αN / ln(1/Pfa) ) — how far above the known-noise ideal the adaptive threshold must sit because 16 cells is a small jury. 2 dB ≈ 11% of detection range, paid everywhere, always.

So why not N = 200? Because the window is a bet that the world is homogeneous across it — and the world has edges. Next slide.

2.4 CA-CFAR

Two blind spots, both measured in the homework

Clutter edges. A CUT just inside a 30 dB clutter wall has half its jury still voting "cheap clear-side noise" → threshold too low → false alarms spray from the edge (measured: ~13× design Pfa in the edge zone — and worse with a longer window). Just past the far edge: the mirror image — an over-thresholded blind strip. The homework plants a 15 dB target there; your detector walks past it.

Masking. Two drones six cells apart: the strong one sits in the weak one's training window, inflates its noise estimate ~10 dB, and the weak drone — detectable alone — vanishes. The jury was packed.

Fixes exist — smallest-of, censored, OS-CFAR (order-statistic) — each buys robustness with extra loss or new edge behavior. Homework Q4: pick one, price it.

The interface you build this week — ca_cfar(power_profile, n_train, n_guard, pfa) — is imported verbatim by lecture 15's range-Doppler pipeline.
