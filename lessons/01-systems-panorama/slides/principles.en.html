1.3 Decibels done right

The grammar — two legal moves and a felony

dBm + dB = dBm   (power through a gain — legal)
dBm − dBm = dB   (two powers compared — legal, yields a ratio)

dBm + dBm = nonsense. Physics does not multiply two powers.

War story: a satellite terminal, one spreadsheet cell — Ptx entered in dBW (dB re 1 watt), read as dBm. 30 dB — a factor of 1000 — hiding in plain sight, found on the roof, not in review. This course's fix: the unit lives in the name — p_t_dbm, p_t_w, fspl_db. Hour 3 re-enacts this exact bug.

1.4 The Friis transmission formula

Spreading × aperture — derived in three steps

  1. Isotropic spreading: density S = Pt/4πR²
  2. A real antenna concentrates: S = PtGt/4πR²
  3. The receiver catches its aperture's worth:
    Ae = Grλ²/4π  →  Pr = S·Ae
Pr = Pt Gt Gr λ² / (4πR)²

G = antenna gain (how much better than isotropic); Ae = effective aperture, m². L13 proves Ae = Gλ²/4π.

1.4 The Friis transmission formula

In dB, as engineers write it

Pr[dBm] = Pt[dBm] + Gt[dBi] + Gr[dBi] − FSPL[dB]

FSPL (free-space path loss) = 20·log₁₀(4πRf/c) — and the anchor number: FSPL(1 GHz, 1 km) = 92.45 dB.

The λ² is not space eating your signal. Free space is lossless — hour 3 measures it. The λ² lives in the receive aperture: fixed gain shrinks with wavelength. Fix the dish size instead and higher f improves the link.

2.1 The noise floor

What the receiver is up against

N[dBm] = −174 + 10·log₁₀(B) + NF
  • Every resistor at temperature T delivers kTB of noise power (Johnson–Nyquist; fine print in L10).
  • At the IEEE reference T₀ = 290 K: kT₀ = −173.98 dBm/Hz.
  • NF (noise figure): the SNR your receiver steals, in dB. Paid once, off the top. Where it comes from — and how cascaded stages share blame — is lecture 10.

Bandwidth is a tax: every ×10 of B raises the floor 10 dB. But B also buys information (comms) and range resolution (radar, L15: ΔR = c/2B). Two masters — homework Q4 makes you defend a choice.

2.2 The radar equation

Five physical steps — watch the R² become R⁴

  1. Density at the target: PtG/4πR²
  2. Target intercepts & re-radiates: ×σ (RCS, m²)
  3. The echo spreads back: ÷4πR² again
  4. The dish catches Ae = Gλ²/4π (same dish — monostatic)
  5. Real radars leak: ÷L (system losses)
Pr = PtG²λ²σ / ((4π)³R⁴L)
2.2 The radar equation

The inverse question — and the exponent that runs the course

Rmax = [ PtG²λ²σ / ((4π)³ kT₀B·F·L·SNRmin) ]1/4

Everything enters at the fourth root.
Double the range → pay 12.04 dB (one-way: 6.02).
Double the power → gain 19% range (21/4 = 1.189).
Cut σ by 4000× → lose only 7.95× range.

Fourth roots make radar upgrades brutally expensive — and stealth merely worthwhile, not magical. The homework's Q2 lives here.

2.2 Radar cross section

  • Fluctuation, Swerling models, what "≈" hides: lecture 14.

The drone problem in one row: a drone and a bird are the same size in σ. Telling them apart is L15's micro-Doppler story — today we only ask whether we can see it at all.
