1.2 The measured two-port

Where the worldview cracks — know it or ship failures

Large signals.

S-parameters are the small-signal limit. Compression, IP3 (third-order intercept): outside the file — lecture 10's territory.

Other bias, other temperature.

That's why the zip has 18 files. μ at 3 V is not μ at 5 V.

Outside the measured band.

The file starts at 10 MHz — and your bias network lives below it. A file cannot clear what it cannot see.

Noise.

A Touchstone .s2p carries no noise data. Four extra numbers per frequency needed (§2.3); this week's set is instructor-modeled, labeled as such.

1.3 The gain zoo

Three gains — and which one is honest

Device S between source ΓS and load ΓL (complex reflection coefficients — the matches you will build choose them).

GT = PL/Pavs  transducer gain — what the amplifier does for you (charges both mismatches)
GA = Pavn/Pavs  available gain — depends only on ΓS → the LNA designer's tool
GP = PL/Pin  power gain — depends only on ΓL

|S₂₁|² = GT with ΓS = ΓL = 0. The datasheet's "gain 11.0 dB" is a 50 Ω fixture number — it is |S₂₁|², nothing deeper.

1.3 The gain zoo · first principles

Mason on the two-port flow graph (lecture 4's investment)

One loop closes first: S₂₂ΓL. The one-loop rule gives the input reflection:

Γin = S₁₁ + S₁₂S₂₁ΓL / (1 − S₂₂ΓL)

Direct reflection, plus a wave that went through, bounced off the load, and leaked back through S₁₂. The blue term seeds everything in hour 2 — stability lives in it.

1.3 The gain zoo

The master formula — every factor a tax or a refund

GT = (1 − |ΓS|²) |S₂₁|² (1 − |ΓL|²) / ( |1 − ΓinΓS|² |1 − S₂₂ΓL|² )
  • Limit check: ΓS = ΓL = 0 → GT = |S₂₁|²  (the 50 Ω special case)
  • Limit check: |ΓS| → 1 → GT → 0  (a reactive source delivers nothing)
  • The homework's cascade referee measures this formula against a built amplifier — L-sections ** device ** L-sections in scikit-rf.

1.4 Stability

Where oscillation physically comes from

Γin = S₁₁ + S₁₂S₂₁ΓL / (1 − S₂₂ΓL)
  • S₁₂: output leaking back to the input (in a FET — field-effect transistor — mostly the gate–drain capacitance). S₂₁ amplifies; ΓL reflects; S₁₂ closes the loop.
  • For some passive loads the term grows until |Γin| > 1: the port reflects more than arrives — negative resistance.
  • Attach any resonator — bias choke, cable length, next stage's filter — and you have built an oscillator.

Hour 3, on this very device: a passive load |ΓL| = 0.40 drives |Γin| to 1.061 — at 10 MHz, a frequency your 2.4 GHz bench never displays.

1.4 Stability

Two tests, one truth

Unconditionally stable at f: |Γin| < 1 and |Γout| < 1 for every passive ΓS, ΓL. Otherwise: conditionally stable — some passive termination oscillates.

K = (1 − |S₁₁|² − |S₂₂|² + |Δ|²) / (2|S₁₂S₂₁|),   Δ = S₁₁S₂₂ − S₁₂S₂₁
Rollett: unconditional ⇔ K > 1 and |Δ| < 1  (two conditions)

μ = (1 − |S₁₁|²) / ( |S₂₂ − Δ·conj(S₁₁)| + |S₁₂S₂₁| )
Edwards–Sinsky 1992: unconditional ⇔ μ > 1  (one condition — and it's a distance, §2.1)

Equivalent theorems — your homework proves they agree at all 660 frequencies. Disagreement = your bug, not Rollett's.

1.4 Stability

The verdict at f₀ — and a war story

K = 1.0973

> 1 ✓

|Δ| = 0.5499

< 1 ✓

μ = 1.2254

> 1 ✓ — unconditionally stable at 2.4 GHz. Barely.

War story: a 2 m-band preamp that oscillated at 40 MHz — only with certain antennas, only cold mornings. Stability had been checked at 144 MHz. At 40 MHz: gain +10 dB, μ = 0.5; the antenna's out-of-band reflection walked into the unstable region as the cable contracted. Fix: one resistor. Lesson: a sweep, not a spot check.

1.5 Recap

Hour 1 in three sentences

  • The .s2p is the device — complete for linear design; silent on large signals, other bias, and noise.
  • GT is the honest gain; |S₂₁|² is its 50 Ω special case — matching buys the gap, up to a ceiling.
  • Stability is a promise about every passive termination — K–Δ or μ, and the violation is an oscillator you didn't order.

2.1 Stability circles

|Γin| = 1 is a circle — and μ is a distance

CL = conj(S₂₂ − Δ·conj(S₁₁)) / (|S₂₂|² − |Δ|²)
RL = |S₁₂S₂₁| / | |S₂₂|² − |Δ|² |

Load plane; swap ports for the source circle. Safe side = the side containing ΓL = 0 (since Γin(0) = S₁₁, |S₁₁| < 1).

Edwards–Sinsky's theorem: μ = the distance from chart center to the nearest unstable load. μ = 0.294 at 10 MHz: a passive load 0.294 out can oscillate. μ = 1.2254 at f₀: the nearest killer is 0.225 outside the chart — unreachable.

2.1 The audit

Sweep μ over the whole file. Every design. Every time.

bandworst μverdict
0.010–0.110 GHz0.294conditional
0.11–15.1 GHz (incl. f₀)≥ 1unconditional
15.1–16.8 GHz0.710conditional

Low band: gain rises as f falls → the S₁₂S₂₁ loop strengthens. High band: |S₂₂| swells out of band (package territory) — the one nobody predicts. Homework Q1 asks you to predict before sweeping.

What "conditional" costs: nothing, if you control the terminations. In-band you do (your match). Out of band — antenna, bias tee, next stage — you don't.

Standard medicine (Steer/Pozar): a small series/shunt R where it hurts noise least, or feedback — a fraction of a dB of gain for μ > 1 everywhere.

2.2 Designing for gain

The simultaneous conjugate match — where MAG lives

ΓS = conj(Γin) and ΓL = conj(Γout), solved together (each side moves the other's target — S₁₂ again). With B₁ = 1 + |S₁₁|² − |S₂₂|² − |Δ|², C₁ = S₁₁ − Δ·conj(S₂₂) (B₂, C₂ port-swapped):

ΓMS = (B₁ − √(B₁² − 4|C₁|²)) / 2C₁    ΓML = (B₂ − √(B₂² − 4|C₂|²)) / 2C₂

Minus root = the one inside the chart. Only meaningful where μ > 1. At 2.4 GHz: ΓMS = 0.413∠−160.8°, ΓML = 0.360∠+70.4° — and the identity GT(ΓMS, ΓML) = MAG is a line your checker measures at 1e-15.

2.2 Designing for gain

MAG is real. MSG is a promise about surgery.

MAG = |S₂₁/S₁₂| · (K − √(K² − 1))  maximum available gain — exists only where K > 1

MSG = |S₂₁/S₁₂|  maximum stable gain — the ceiling you'd have after stabilizing to exactly K = 1
Datasheets quote MSG in the conditional region. This device "offers" 12.128 dB at 2.4 GHz; only MAG's 10.227 is real as-is. The homework makes you print both and say which is honest where.

Course ledger note: MAG = GT,max = GA,max = GP,max — at the simultaneous match all three gains coincide.

2.2 Designing for gain

You almost never design at MAG — the −2 dB procedure

At MAG every margin is zero: tolerance walks the match, gain sags, stability margin evaporates (remember "barely"). The homework's target: MAG − 2 dB = 8.227 dB.

1. Draw the constant-GA = target circle in the ΓS plane.
2. Pick a point ΓS on it — which point is a free choice (next slide spends it).
3. Conjugate-match the output: ΓL = conj(Γout(ΓS)).
→ GT = GA = target, exactly.

Reference pick when nothing else matters: the circle point nearest the chart center — the gentlest network.

The referee is a build, not a formula: lecture 3's L-sections realized in skrf, cascaded, |S₂₁|² read at f₀ — target hit to 1e-13 dB or your algebra is wrong.

2.3 Noise circles

NF depends on the source match — four numbers per frequency

F = Fmin + 4·rn·|ΓS − Γopt|² / ( (1 − |ΓS|²)·|1 + Γopt|² )
  • Fmin — the floor (here 0.820 dB at 2.4 GHz) · Γopt — the source reflection that reaches it (0.372∠75.6°) · rn = Rn/Z₀ — how steeply F rises as you leave (0.136)
  • Constant-F loci: circles nested around Γopt — the third circle family on the same chart.

Provenance, honestly: .s2p files carry no noise data; Γopt needs a source-pull bench. This week's noise parameters are instructor-modeled, calibrated to reproduce the PGA-103+ datasheet's 50 Ω NF column at ΓS = 0. Teaching data, labeled as such.

2.3 Noise circles

The tension that defines the LNA

Gain wants ΓS = ΓMS (0.413∠−161°). Noise wants ΓS = Γopt (0.372∠+76°). Different points — you cannot have both.

sit atG_TNF
Γ_MS10.227 dB1.599 dB
Γ_opt8.794 dB0.820 dB

Noise match costs 1.433 dB of gain; gain match costs 0.78 dB of noise. Every LNA ever designed lives between these endpoints.

2.3 The frontier

Pick a point — then defend it with lecture 10

  • Module 3: walk ΓS from ΓMS to Γopt, output re-matched each step → the gain-vs-NF frontier (monotone both ways — your checker counts violations).
  • Defense = Friis with a second stage (NF₂ = 6 dB):
    system_nf_db(1.599, 10.227, 6.0) = 2.376 dB (gain end)
    system_nf_db(0.820,  8.794, 6.0) = 2.045 dB (noise end) — noise end wins by 0.33 dB; flips if NF₂ ≳ 8.7 dB.

Quiet punchline (homework Q2): this device's noise match still clears the MAG − 2 dB gain target — the dreaded trade costs less than the humility you already budgeted. Common in practice; nobody tells students.

2.4 Bias — one slide, as promised

Bias is part of the termination

  • Choke = feed DC, block RF. Blocks: the file's ports are DC-open by convention.
  • Rule 1: the bias network is part of ΓL — chokes are inductors, inductors resonate, and 10 MHz (μ = 0.294!) is bias-network country.
  • Rule 2: the vendor's "suggested layout" exists because ground inductance eats stability margins.

Full bias design is a lab course; its stability consequences are this course.

2.4 The complete single-stage LNA procedure

The checklist the homework executes end-to-end

1. Measured S (+ noise) data at your bias.  2. Sweep K–Δ and μ over the whole file; find every μ < 1 band.  3. At f₀: μ > 1 → MAG exists; else stabilize, re-audit.  4. Target below the ceiling (MAG − 2 dB).  5. GA circles + noise circles on one chart; pick ΓS on the frontier, knowingly.  6. ΓL = conj(Γout).  7. Realize both matches (L-sections; stubs at higher f).  8. Verify by cascade: GT(f₀), swept response, and the finished amp's |Γin|, |Γout| over the whole file.  9. Bias without ruining step 8.

The misconception half the room holds: "unconditionally stable = stable at the design frequency." No — it means every passive termination at that frequency, and says nothing about 10 MHz. Hour 3 rebuilds that exact failure, on purpose.

2.5 Recap

Hour 2 in three sentences

  • Stability circles put the danger on the chart; μ is your distance from it — sweep the whole file, always.
  • Gain design: a ceiling (MAG = 10.227 dB), a humble target (−2 dB), a circle to pick from — refereed by a built cascade.
  • Noise adds circles around a different center; the LNA is standing between the two centers with your eyes open — and Friis (L10) picks the spot.
