1.1 One transmitter, four antennas

The humble box this week is about

Lecture 13's array, fed. The demands:

  • Equal amplitude, equal phase — errors become sidelobes
  • Matched everywhere
  • Isolated outputs — one element's reflection must not re-emerge in a neighbor's drive

The humble two-way splitter that does all this is provably impossible with three ports. This lecture: the proof, and the two escapes.

1.1 Why this lecture exists

Three claims for today

  1. A three-port cannot be matched, reciprocal, and lossless at once.
    Pick two. Three lines of lecture-4 algebra; hour 3 prints its fingerprint: 1.000000.
  2. Even/odd analysis is the technique of the week.
    Four lines dissolve the Wilkinson, six the branch-line — and it returns for lecture 9's coupled lines.
  3. Couplers are specified by C, D, I — and two of them are routinely confused.
    Isolation ≠ directivity; they differ by exactly the coupling, and the confusion has a price tag.
1.2 The impossibility theorem

Matched + reciprocal + lossless: the fast proof

Assume all three of a 3×3 S-matrix (lecture 4's language):

S₁₁ = S₂₂ = S₃₃ = 0  ·  S = Sᵀ  ·  SᴴS = I

Column orthogonality (zeros do the work):  S₁₃*S₂₃ = 0,   S₁₂*S₂₃ = 0,   S₁₂*S₁₃ = 0
→ at least two of the three entries vanish.
Column norms: |S₁₂|² + |S₁₃|² = 1 (and cyclic) → some column has norm 0 ≠ 1. Contradiction.

Three lines. When a vendor claims a matched, lossless, reciprocal three-way splitter, one of those words is marketing.

1.2 The impossibility theorem

The same proof, said physically

  • Unit column norm = power in at one port all comes out — losslessness, one port at a time.
  • Column orthogonality = losslessness for combined drives: superposition must conserve power for every excitation, not just the ones in the test plan.
  • The match assumption deleted the diagonal — orthogonality runs out of degrees of freedom.

No topology escapes. The proof never assumed one — no arrangement of lossless plumbing behind three matched ports can dodge it.

Hold "every excitation, not just the test plan" — it returns as homework Q3, and as a war story.

1.3 Pick two

The matched resistive star — honesty in a can

Matched everywhere, reciprocal, S₁₂ = S₁₃ = S₂₃ = ½:

  • Every path: −6.02 dB — 3 dB honest split + 3 dB heat
  • Outputs hear each other at −6 dB — no isolation

Fine as a lab divider. As an array feed: disqualified twice — loss and crosstalk.

1.4 The Wilkinson divider

Wilkinson 1960 — the whole miracle is the placement

  • Two quarter-wave arms, √2·Z₀ each
  • One resistor between the outputs — not to ground
  • Symmetric about the horizontal mirror → even/odd analysis applies

Claimed: matched at all 3 ports, reciprocal, outputs isolated, and the 1→2/3 split lossless. The theorem's bill gets paid — but by whom?

1.4 The technique of the week

Even/odd: mirror, cut, superpose

Any drive of the symmetric pair = even part (+V, +V) + odd part (+V, −V): (a₂, a₃) = ½(a₂+a₃)·(1,1) + ½(a₂−a₃)·(1,−1)

Solve two half-sized problems, then:  S₂₂ = ½(Γe + Γo),   S₂₃ = ½(Γe − Γo). That sum-and-difference line is the whole method — and it returns for lecture 9's coupled lines.

1.4 The Wilkinson, worked

Even mode matches the arm; odd mode is the resistor

Even (from output 2):

  • λ/4 of 70.7 Ω into 2Z₀ = 100 Ω
  • Zin = 70.7²/100 = 50 Ω → Γe = 0
  • R/2 dangles, both ends at equal volts:
    the resistor is invisible to the even mode

Odd (from output 2):

  • shorted λ/4 arm → open. Gone.
  • only R/2 to ground remains
  • match ⇔ R/2 = Z₀

R = 2Z₀ = 100 Ω

not a tweak — the odd-mode match, full stop

Superpose: Γe = Γo = 0 → S₂₂ = 0 and S₂₃ = 0: isolated outputs.
Input: two λ/4 branches → 100 Ω ∥ 100 Ω = 50 Ω → S₁₁ = 0.
Through: S₂₁ = S₃₁ = −j/√2 — 3.01 dB at −90°.

1.4 The Wilkinson vs the theorem

Matched, reciprocal, isolated — so where is the loss?

S(f₀) = 0−j/√2−j/√2 −j/√200 −j/√200

general (Z₂, R): Γe = (Z₂²−2Z₀²)/(Z₂²+2Z₀²), Γo = (R−2Z₀)/(R+2Z₀), S₂₁ = −2jZ₀Z₂/(Z₂²+2Z₀²) — homework module 1

‖SᴴS − I‖ = 1.000000 at f₀. Not lossless — the theorem holds. Hour 3 prints this number.

Drive port 1, balanced & matched: pure even mode, no resistor current, zero dissipation. Drive an output: half crosses, the odd half dies in R. The loss is aimed at the excitations you never use — imbalance and reflections. A bouncer, not a toll.

War story: the 2 W isolation resistor that "never dissipates anything" — until one of two 100 W PAs (power amplifiers) died, and 50 W arrived. Rate the bouncer for the fight.

1.5 Recap

Hour 1 in three sentences

  • A matched, reciprocal, lossless three-port is a theorem-level impossibility — pick two, and know which two your vendor picked.
  • Even/odd = mirror + superpose: even sees an open, odd sees a short; S-entries are half-sums and half-differences.
  • The Wilkinson hides the mandatory loss in a resistor only the odd mode can see — R = 2Z₀ is the odd-mode match — and hands you −j/√2.

Break. Hour 2: buy a fourth port instead — and the theorem changes sides.

Hour 2

Four-Ports:
Couplers, Hybrids, and Where They Live

C, D, I said honestly · the 90° and 180° families · coupled lines · array feeds, balanced amps, monopulse

2.1 The directional coupler

C, D, I — three numbers, one identity

I = C + D

Coupling C = −20·log|S₃₁| — how big the tap is (3, 10, 20 dB)

Isolation I = −20·log|S₄₁| — total leakage to the quiet port

Directivity D = I − C — how well it tells forward from backward. The quality number.

"Isolation = directivity" is wrong for any coupler whose coupling isn't 0 dB. They differ by exactly C.

Bonus theorem: a matched, reciprocal, lossless four-port is possible — and is necessarily a directional coupler. What three ports forbid, four ports mandate.

2.1 Directivity in the wild

D is the ceiling of what a reflectometer can certify

Coupled port hears two things:

  • the echo: reflected wave via C — the signal
  • the forward wave via I = C + D — the leakage

Leakage = echo when RL (return loss) = D. Readings beyond D are fiction — the phasor sum can say anything.

technologytypical D
machined waveguide30–40 dB
stripline20–30 dB
edge-coupled microstrip10–15 dB

Why microstrip is the sad row: §2.4. Lecture 9 pays the bill.

War story: a 12-dB-directivity reflectometer read a "26 dB" return loss at site acceptance. A proper bridge later: 14 dB. Nobody lied — at RL ≈ D the echo and the leakage are the same size.

2.2 The branch-line hybrid

A square of quarter-waves — even/odd, second performance

Same ritual: bisect; even → λ/8 open stubs (+jY₀), odd → λ/8 shorted stubs (−jY₀); each half is a two-port, carry Γ and T:

S₁₁ = ½(Γₑ+Γₒ) · S₂₁ = ½(Tₑ+Tₒ)
S₃₁ = ½(Tₑ−Tₒ) · S₄₁ = ½(Γₑ−Γₒ)

Both halves reflectionless; Tₑ = −(1+j)/√2, Tₒ = (1−j)/√2.

2.2 The branch-line hybrid

The quadrature fingerprint — and the bandwidth bill

S₁₁ = 0   S₂₁ = −j/√2   S₃₁ = −1/√2   S₄₁ = 0

Equal 3.01 dB split, outputs 90° apart — the quadrature (90°) hybrid. Which input condition nulls what: §2.3, and homework Q2.

Everything leaned on "λ/4 at f₀." Hour 3 measures the decay:

  • balance within 0.5 dB: 9.10–10.90 GHz — 18% fractional bandwidth
  • directivity at 9.5 GHz: 17.6 dB; at 8 GHz: 6.8 dB

Hybrids are narrowband creatures. Multisection versions buy octaves — lecture 6's Chebyshev instinct, applied to couplers.

2.3 The 180° family

The rat-race — Σ and Δ are born

Drive ports 2 and 3 with a and b:

port 1 → (a + b)/√2 = Σ
port 4 → (b − a)/√2 = Δ

Equal, in-phase inputs: Σ takes everything, Δ takes exactly nothing — the most valuable zero in radar (§2.5).

Waveguide twin: the magic tee (E-arm/H-arm) — same algebra, different plumbing.

2.3 The taxonomy that matters

90° hybrids and 180° hybrids are different tools

90° family — branch-line, coupled-line, Lange

outputs in quadrature; reflections cancel toward the isolated port — balanced amplifiers live here (L11)

180° family — rat-race, magic tee

outputs in/anti phase; sums and differences — monopulse comparators live here (L16)

Both split 3 dB. What differs is the phase the two internal paths carry — and therefore which input condition produces the null. Homework Q2 asks you to predict a 90° hybrid's null position before the code answers. Commit in ink.

2.4 Coupled-line couplers

Two traces, close together — even/odd impedances as the design space

Different field pictures → different impedances. That pair is the coupler.

match ⇔ Z₀ₑ·Z₀ₒ = Z₀²  ·  C₀ = (Z₀ₑ−Z₀ₒ)/(Z₀ₑ+Z₀ₒ)
CZ₀ₑZ₀ₒ
20 dB55.3 Ω45.2 Ωeasy
10 dB69.4 Ω36.0 Ωintimate

Microstrip's even and odd modes travel at different speeds (odd lives more in air) → imperfect cancellation → the 10–15 dB directivity of §2.1. Stripline (true TEM — transverse electromagnetic) is immune. Lecture 9 inherits both the gift and the bill.

2.5 Where they live

Corporate feeds and balanced amplifiers

Corporate array feed — the homework

three Wilkinsons, equal path lengths, isolation contains each element's reflections. Errors grow with tree depth (log₂N), not element count — Q1 makes you predict how, in ink, before the instrument shows you.

Balanced amplifier — lecture 11's escape hatch

two amplifier copies between two quadrature hybrids: each amp's reflection routes to the terminated port, not the input. The pair is matched even though each half is not — the hybrid's resistor quietly eats the difference.

Same two parts, two industries: the Wilkinson feeds apertures; the 90° hybrid launders reflections. Both are "aim the loss at the signal you never want."

2.5 The radar tie-in

The monopulse comparator — angle from a single pulse

  • Four antenna quadrants, four 180° hybrids
  • Boresight: both Δ ports are nulls
  • Off axis: the subtraction un-cancels; Δ/Σ reads the angle error — sign and size, one pulse

The null is sharper than the beam — how fire-control radars track to a fraction of a beamwidth. Software version: lecture 16.

Δ_az / Δ_el: azimuth / elevation difference channels.

2.6 Recap

Hour 2 in four sentences

  • Four-ports legalize what three-ports forbid — and the legal device is exactly the directional coupler.
  • I = C + D: C is the tap's size, D its honesty — never let a datasheet blur them.
  • The 90° family and the 180° family split identically and null differently.
  • Coupled lines make even/odd impedances the design variables — lecture 9's filters are waiting.
